What you get

The times of day direct sun reaches each street-facing wall and the tables in front, whether it is sunny right now, a shadow map you can play through the day, and the sunniest cafés nearby.

How it works

Where do the numbers come from?

Building footprints and heights from OpenStreetMap within 250 m. Every 5 minutes from sunrise to sunset, a line is traced from each wall sample and each table spot to the sun; if a building blocks it, that minute is shade. Sun position and shadow maths match the cityshade reference model to about five minutes.

What is not included?

Trees, awnings, umbrellas, hills and weather. It is direct sun on a clear day over flat ground. Where OpenStreetMap has no building height, a typical local height is assumed; the page says how much was real and lets you change it.

Flats, hotels, roofs and events

Pick what you are checking in the dropdown. Hotels and rentals get sun hours on the windows of every floor plus a listing sentence. Roofs get an unshaded share by month for a solar pre-check. Events get sun and shade for a start and end time, with sun direction and golden hour.
