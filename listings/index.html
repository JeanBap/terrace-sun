API reference

GET /api/v1/sun · JSON · CORS enabled · results cached 30 days per location and floor · OpenAPI spec

Parameters

lat, londecimal degrees, required
floor0 = ground. Default 0
floorsfloors in the building, overrides the OpenStreetMap height
heightassumed height in metres for untagged neighbours
namelabel echoed into the report link
yearreference year, default current
keyAPI key, or send Authorization: Bearer

Errors

400 bad coordinates · 401 missing key · 403 invalid key · 503 calculating: a new location's building data is still arriving; the calculation finishes in the background, retry after retry_after_s (usually 20 s). The badge and the report never wait for this: they compute in the visitor's browser if needed.

Questions

What is the Sun Score?

The share of daylight hours with direct sun on the sunniest street-facing wall of the home at its floor, averaged over 20 March, 21 June, 22 September and 21 December. 70 or more is A, 55 is B, 40 is C, 25 is D.

Where does the data come from?

Building footprints and heights from OpenStreetMap within 250 m, a solar position model accurate to a few minutes, and 2.5D shadow casting every 10 minutes from sunrise to sunset. Trees, awnings and terrain are not included, and the report says so.

What if OpenStreetMap has no height for the building?

The report says which figures are estimated. You set the floor count with floors, which fixes the building itself. Neighbours use a typical local height unless you pass height.

Is this legally safe to publish?

It is a calculated estimate from open data with the method and limits printed on the report, not a survey. Keep the disclaimer and let buyers re-run the link. That is more than any "luminoso" claim can offer.

Which countries?

Anywhere OpenStreetMap has buildings, which is most European and North American cities. Coverage of heights varies; Italy, Germany, France, Spain and the UK are good in city centres.
