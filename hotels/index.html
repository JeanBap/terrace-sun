Terrace Sun for hotels

Which rooms get the morning sun? Is the roof terrace still lit at aperitivo time? Type the hotel name or address, then pick the floor.

Click anywhere on the map to check that spot. Coloured dots are nearby cafés, brighter means more sun.

How it works

  1. Search the hotel and click its building on the map.
  2. Read the sun times for each street-facing wall: that is the light through the windows on that side.
  3. Open "Apartment or house", pick a floor, and copy the sentence into the room description.
  4. Use 21 June and 21 December to describe summer and winter honestly.

You get: the exact sun times per wall and per terrace, whether it is sunny right now, a shadow map you can play through the day, monthly totals, and a ranking of the sunniest cafés within 220 m.

Or use the general café and terrace tool.

Questions

How do I price rooms by sun?

Rooms on a wall with 6 h or more of direct sun in winter are the ones guests remember. Name them ("the morning-sun room"), photograph them at the lit time shown here, and price the difference.

What about the breakfast terrace?

Click the terrace itself, not the building. The "Tables" row gives the sun times at table height. Check it for the breakfast window on 21 December, the worst case.

How does Terrace Sun work out when the sun hits a café?

It fetches building footprints and heights from OpenStreetMap, finds the café's building and its street-facing walls, then traces a ray towards the sun every five minutes to check whether a taller building blocks it.

What is the difference between "wall" and "tables"?

"Wall" is the café's façade at 1.5 m above the pavement, lit only when the sun is in front of it. "Tables" is a point 2.5 m out from the wall at table height, which can also get sun from the side.

Can I use it for an apartment or house listing?

Yes. Search the address or click the building. The "Apartment or house" card shows sun hours on the windows of every floor, from ground floor to the top, for each street-facing wall. Pick the floor of the flat, choose a date (21 December is the honest worst case), and use "Copy listing text" for a sentence you can paste into the listing.

Does it account for trees, awnings or clouds?

No. It shows direct sun on a clear day with flat ground. Trees, umbrellas, awnings, hills and weather are not included, so real shade in leafy streets will be greater.

How accurate are the times?

The sun position and shadow maths match the cityshade reference model to within about five minutes. The main error source is missing building heights in OpenStreetMap. The page tells you how much height data exists nearby and lets you change the assumed height.
